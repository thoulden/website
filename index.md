# Research

<div class="publication">

  <p class="publication-title">
    <strong>When Does Automating AI Research Produce Explosive Growth?
    Feedback Loops in Innovation Networks</strong>
    <em>(May, 2026)</em>
  </p>

  <p class="publication-links">
    <a href="/assets/DHHK_oct26.pdf">[working paper]</a>,
    <a href="https://thoulden.github.io/DHHK_BGP_Calculator">[simulator]</a>,
    <a href="https://www.nber.org/papers/w35155">[NBER (older version)]</a>,
    <a href="https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/ai-feedback-loops-and-conditions-explosive-growth">[VoxEU]</a>
    <span class="publication-authors">
      (with Tom Davidson, Basil Halperin, and Anton Korinek)
    </span>
  </p>

  <details class="abstract-dropdown">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>
      AI labs are increasingly using AI itself to accelerate AI research,
      creating a feedback loop that could lead to an intelligence explosion.
      We develop a general semi-endogenous growth model with an innovation
      network, where research and automation in one sector increase the
      productivity of research in other sectors, and derive a clean analytical
      condition under which growth becomes superexponential ("explosive").
      We find that automating research can offset diminishing returns to ideas
      by activating two reinforcing channels: a technological feedback loop
      across research sectors, and an economic feedback loop in which higher
      output finances further research. Growth becomes explosive if the
      combined strength of technological and economic feedback loops
      overcomes diminishing returns. In a simple simulation calibrated to
      trends in AI progress, fully automating software research and modest
      (5%) automation in other sectors generates a singularity within six
      years. Bottlenecks do not overturn the result if task automation
      advances sufficiently fast.
    </p>
  </details>

</div>

## Other Work

<div class="publication">

  <p class="publication-title">
    <strong>Aggregating Slow and Fast Growth</strong>
    <em>(March, 2026)</em>
  </p>

  <p class="publication-links">
    <a href="/assets/AggregatingGrowth.pdf">[note]</a>
  </p>

  <details class="abstract-dropdown">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <p>
      In this note I consider an economy with two factor inputs, one
      growing exponentially and one growing hyperbolically. I derive
      the conditions under which aggregate output is growing
      superexponentially versus subexponentially. In the case where the
      slow-growing factor is fixed, I obtain a closed-form solution for
      the date at which the growth regime changes from superexponential
      to subexponential.
    </p>
  </details>

</div>
